03 — RESEARCH INTERESTS

My research interests lie in human–AI interaction, particularly how AI agents can become reliable collaborators that help people reason with evidence. I am interested in how users communicate their beliefs and preferences, how agents respond, and how these interactions shape users’ judgments and reliance on AI.

I am also interested in AI for social science and mobile health: using AI to study human beliefs and behavior, while designing interactions that help people understand personal and wearable health data. I am especially curious about how agents can respect users’ experiences without simply reinforcing their existing views.

I hope to combine controlled model evaluations, interactive prototypes, and user studies to explore grounding, preference alignment, and appropriate reliance in human–agent collaboration.

04 — ONGOING RESEARCH

IN PROGRESS · COLUMBIA UNIVERSITY

Same Data, Different Judgments? Sycophancy in LLM Interpretations of Wearable Health Data

Advised by Xia Zhou

This project investigates whether users’ expressed beliefs shift LLM interpretations of identical wearable sleep and activity records, and whether users mistake the model’s agreement for evidence from their health data.

The planned model evaluation compares positive, negative, and neutral user statements while holding the wearable records constant. It examines numerical accuracy, evaluative judgments, uncertainty, and the distinction between acknowledging personal experiences and making unsupported claims.

A proposed interface will separate recorded data, subjective context, and model inferences. A planned user study will examine whether this helps people understand the evidence and identify what information to check next.

Publication goal: CHI or PACM IMWUT (UbiComp). Work in progress; not yet submitted.
